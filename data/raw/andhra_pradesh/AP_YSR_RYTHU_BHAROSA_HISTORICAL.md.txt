## 2. Scheme Overview

YSR Rythu Bharosa was an Andhra Pradesh Government farmer-support
programme introduced under the earlier state government's Navaratnalu
programme.

The programme provided financial assistance to eligible farming
families/cultivators.

The historical scheme included support for:

- Landholding farmer families
- Tenant farmers
- Record of Forest Rights (ROFR) cultivators
- Eligible farming households under the applicable state rules

The scheme was administered through the Agriculture & Cooperation
Department and local agricultural field machinery.

Rythu Bharosa Kendras were used as village-level agricultural service
points, but the RBK network is a separate service-delivery
infrastructure and should not be confused with the financial scheme.


## 3. Historical Target Beneficiaries

The historical YSR Rythu Bharosa framework covered eligible:

- Landholding farmer families
- Tenant farmers
- ROFR cultivators
- Other cultivators covered by the applicable state rules

The exact beneficiary categories and payment amounts changed across
different years.

Therefore the RAG must retain academic/financial year metadata.


## 4. Historical Eligibility Criteria

### Citizenship / Residence

Historical scheme guidelines required the beneficiary to be a
permanent resident of Andhra Pradesh.


### Farmer / Cultivator Status

The beneficiary had to satisfy the applicable farmer/cultivator
definition.

Historical implementation included:

- Landholding farmers
- Tenant farmers with Crop Cultivator Rights Card (CCRC)
- ROFR cultivators


### Income

Historical scheme documentation contained an income ceiling.

One official Andhra Pradesh scheme SOP specifies:

Total family income:
Less than ₹10,000 per month.

However, this is a historical rule and must not be treated as the
current eligibility rule for Annadata Sukhibhava–PM KISAN.


### Land

Historical YSR Rythu Bharosa eligibility rules contained landholding
and government-beneficiary exclusions.

The exact thresholds varied according to the applicable government
guidelines/version.

Do not use a single land ceiling as a timeless rule.


### Government Employment

Historical eligibility rules contained exclusions related to
government employment/pension and other specified categories.

These were part of the historical scheme eligibility framework.


### Income Tax

Historical eligibility rules included exclusion of specified
income-tax-paying households/categories.


### Other Exclusions

Historical scheme rules also included exclusions relating to:

- Institutional landholders
- Certain government/public-sector employees
- Certain elected representatives
- Other categories specified in the eligibility guidelines

These exclusions are historical and should not be transferred to
the current Annadata Sukhibhava engine without separate verification.


## 5. Historical Exclusions / Non-Eligibility

Depending on the scheme year, historical YSR Rythu Bharosa rules
excluded categories such as:

- Non-eligible non-farmer households
- Institutional landholders
- Certain government employees
- Certain pensioners
- Income-tax payers
- Certain elected representatives
- Households exceeding prescribed land/income conditions
- Other categories specified in the applicable government rules

Because these conditions were version-specific, the RAG should store
them under the relevant scheme year.


## 6. Historical Benefits

### Financial Assistance

The original YSR Rythu Bharosa programme was announced as:

₹50,000 financial assistance over five years.

The later annual assistance structure was:

₹12,500 per farmer family per year.

Subsequent government orders modified the amount and beneficiary
coverage.

Therefore:

Historical benefit:
VERSION_DEPENDENT.


### Tenant Farmers

Tenant farmers were included under the applicable state component.

CCRC holders were used to establish cultivator status.

### ROFR Cultivators

Record of Forest Rights cultivators were included under the
applicable implementation rules.

### Additional Associated Benefits

The broader YSR Rythu Bharosa programme was associated with
additional agricultural-support initiatives, including:

- Zero-interest crop loans
- Free borewells
- Farmer service infrastructure

These should not be merged into the direct financial-assistance
benefit unless the individual component is separately represented.


## 7. Historical Required Documents

Depending on the beneficiary category, historical documentation
could include:

- Aadhaar
- Rice Card / household information
- Land ownership records
- Cultivation records
- Crop Cultivator Rights Card (CCRC)
- Record of Forest Rights (ROFR) documentation
- Income certificate where required
- Domicile/residence information
- Bank-account details

The exact document set depended on the beneficiary category and
scheme year.


## 8. Historical Application / Verification Process

Historical YSR Rythu Bharosa implementation used a government
verification workflow.

A documented Andhra Pradesh scheme procedure describes:

1. Citizen applies through the Citizen Schemes Portal.
2. Application is forwarded to Agriculture/Horticulture/Sericulture
   Assistant.
3. Field verification is conducted.
4. Application is forwarded to the Mandal Agriculture Officer.
5. MAO verifies and recommends the application.
6. Provisional eligible and reverification lists are published.
7. Citizens can raise grievances against ineligibility.
8. Final eligible/ineligible lists are published.
9. Eligible beneficiaries receive payment through Aadhaar-enabled
   payment systems.

This is the historical YSR Rythu Bharosa process.

Do not present this as the current Annadata Sukhibhava process.

## 12. Related / Replacement Government Programs

### Annadata Sukhibhava–PM KISAN

This is the current Andhra Pradesh farmer investment-support programme.

Current Agriculture Department material states:

- ₹20,000 annual support per eligible farmer under the combined
  state + PM KISAN framework.
- ₹6,000 of this amount comes from PM KISAN.
- The state component covers additional eligible farmer/cultivator
  categories.

Landless cultivators are also described in current Andhra Pradesh
Agriculture Department material as receiving ₹20,000 annual state
assistance.

Annadata Sukhibhava–PM KISAN must be stored as a separate RAG record.


### PM KISAN

PM KISAN is a Government of India scheme.

Its eligibility and exclusions are separate from the historical
YSR Rythu Bharosa rules.


### Rythu Bharosa Kendras

RBKs are village-level agricultural service centres.

They provide services such as:

- Agricultural inputs
- Seeds
- Fertilizers
- Crop advisories
- e-Crop services
- Allied-sector services

RBKs are not themselves the historical YSR Rythu Bharosa cash-benefit
scheme.
